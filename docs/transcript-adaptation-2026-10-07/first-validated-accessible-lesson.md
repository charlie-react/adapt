## European Colonialism in Africa: Control, Division, and Legacy

Colonialism in Africa describes the era when European powers established political, economic, and military control over much of the continent. This control intensified especially from the late 19th century onward. The Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885 marked a major turning point in this process. During this conference, European countries divided African territories among themselves, making decisions with little regard for existing ethnic groups, kingdoms, or boundaries that had been established by African societies.

Colonial governments introduced new legal systems, schools, roads, railways, and administrative structures across the territories they controlled. However, these developments were designed mainly to serve colonial interests rather than the needs of African populations. African land, labor, minerals, and agricultural resources were heavily exploited by the colonial powers. Many local industries and political institutions that had existed before colonization were weakened or dismantled during this period.

Colonial rule created artificial borders that did not reflect the cultural, ethnic, or political realities of African societies. These imposed boundaries later contributed to political and ethnic tensions in the post-colonial period. From the mid 20th century, African nationalist movements grew stronger across the continent. Many countries gained independence during the 1950s and 1960s, ending formal colonial rule. The legacy of colonialism remains important today, continuing to influence Africa's borders, languages, economies, and political systems.

Key points

- European powers took control of African territories, with intensified colonization from the late 19th century.
- The Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885 divided Africa among European countries without considering existing African societies.
- Colonial governments built infrastructure and institutions that primarily served colonial economic and political interests.
- African resources were exploited while local industries and political structures were weakened.
- Artificial colonial borders created lasting political and ethnic tensions.
- Nationalist movements led to independence for many African countries during the 1950s and 1960s, though colonial legacies continue to shape the continent.
