## Colonialism in Africa: Definition and Historical Context

Colonialism in Africa refers to the period when European powers took political, economic, and military control over much of the continent, especially from the late 19th century. A major turning point was the Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885, where European countries divided African territories among themselves with little regard for existing ethnic groups, kingdoms, or boundaries.

### Key points

- European powers took political, economic, and military control over much of the continent, especially from the late 19th century.

- A major turning point was the Berlin Conference of 1884 to 1885, where European countries divided African territories among themselves with little regard for existing ethnic groups, kingdoms, or boundaries.

## Colonial Infrastructure and Interests

Colonial governments introduced new legal systems, schools, roads, railways, and administrative structures. However, these developments mainly served colonial interests.

### Key points

- Colonial governments introduced new legal systems, schools, roads, railways, and administrative structures.

- However, these developments mainly served colonial interests.

## Exploitation and Weakening of Local Systems

African land, labor, minerals, and agricultural resources were heavily exploited, while many local industries and political institutions were weakened. Colonial rule also created artificial borders that later contributed to political and ethnic tensions.

### Key points

- African land, labor, minerals, and agricultural resources were heavily exploited, while many local industries and political institutions were weakened.

- Colonial rule also created artificial borders that later contributed to political and ethnic tensions.

## Independence Movements and Colonial Legacy

From the mid 20th century, African nationalist movements grew stronger, and many countries gained independence during the 1950s and 1960s. The legacy of colonialism remains important today, influencing Africa's borders, languages, economies, and political systems.

### Key points

- From the mid 20th century, African nationalist movements grew stronger, and many countries gained independence during the 1950s and 1960s.

- The legacy of colonialism remains important today, influencing Africa's borders, languages, economies, and political systems.
