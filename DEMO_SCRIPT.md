# ADAPT — 60-second Moonshot demo

Read only the **Say** lines: approximately 150 words, or one minute at a steady pace. Each fallback replaces its beat; it is not extra narration.

Before presenting, rehearse with the actual five-minute recording. Keep all waiting visible. The ten-second result is a previous observation, not a promise for this live run.

## 1. The person and their problem · 0–10 seconds

**Show:** ADAPT's upload screen. Select the lecture recording.

**Say:** Imagine a student who cannot reliably hear a recorded lesson. They need the same knowledge as their classmates, but the recording stands in their way.

**Fallback:** This is the student ADAPT serves: someone who needs another way into the same lesson.

## 2. The moment it hurts · 10–20 seconds

**Show:** Submit the audio; keep the waiting state visible.

**Say:** A five-minute lecture can mean repeated replaying, searching for one important detail. ADAPT gives that student readable learning content they can explore at their own pace.

**Fallback:** Even without a live result, the problem is clear: replaying audio is not an accessible alternative for everyone.

## 3. What I show on screen · 20–30 seconds

**Show:** When available, choose “Make spoken content readable,” then “Create my learning experience.”

**Say:** I upload the recording and choose “Make spoken content readable.” Then I create the learning experience. No diagnosis is needed. No learning objective is required.

**Fallback:** The live service is taking longer; the learner's choices are still simply their material and the support they need.

## 4. The wow moment · 30–40 seconds

**Show:** Only when the lesson is visible, navigate its sections and key points using the keyboard.

**Say:** In my recent tests, this took about ten seconds. Here are readable sections and key points. The learner can move between sections without hearing the recording.

**Fallback:** This run is still waiting; earlier tests took about ten seconds, but I won't present an unfinished lesson as ready.

## 5. How Codex helped · 40–50 seconds

**Show:** Continue navigating the lesson; if necessary, remain on the current screen.

**Say:** Codex helped me plan, build, test and debug ADAPT. I chose the learner needs and reviewed the results. Together, we fixed questions that lacked lesson evidence.

**Fallback:** The live demo can fail; the development work still includes documented debugging, tests and source-preservation reviews with Codex.

## 6. What is next · 50–60 seconds

**Show:** Finish on the learning experience. Point to Read Aloud without promising an untested browser voice.

**Say:** Next, I want students to try it and finish real-device accessibility checks. ADAPT changes how learning happens, not what the learner is expected to learn.

**Fallback:** The next step is the same: student trials and real-device accessibility checks, including audible playback and screen readers.
